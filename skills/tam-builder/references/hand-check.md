# Hand check

The list is not done until a person has checked a sample against the sources. No accuracy number is quoted before this.

1. Take a fixed-seed sample of 25 hot accounts (fixed, so a rerun after fixes checks the same rows). Add every hot row with a confidence flag or an unusual owner.
2. For each, write one line: the single claim the brief leans on most, and the link that supports it. Put them on a one-page checklist the person can tick from a phone.
3. The person opens each link and marks it: **good** (the page says it), **stale** (it was true; the job post has closed, the page changed), or **wrong** (the page does not say it, or it is about another company).
4. For every stale row: rewrite that clause of the brief in the past tense. For every wrong row: find the input or rule that produced it (a mismatched company record, a title that only looks like the buyer, a tool named on a reseller's page), fix that, and rerun stages 4 to 6. Never patch the brief alone.
5. Log the result in the research folder: n checked, good, stale, wrong, what was fixed, which rows moved band.

Common causes of a wrong row:
- the data source matched a different company with a similar name or a shared domain
- a job title that contains the right words but is not the buyer (a recruiter "for" the IT team, an audit role)
- a tool listed on an integrations or marketplace page rather than in use
- a count of mentions treated as a count of people
